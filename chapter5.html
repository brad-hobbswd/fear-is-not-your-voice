Chapter 5

Storm Focus or Savior Focus

Fear trains attention toward waves and uncertainty while faith anchors vision upon the presence and authority of Jesus.

Fear trains the mind to scan constantly for danger. Fear studies conditions carefully. Fear measures every possible outcome. Fear believes survival depends entirely upon vigilance.

Faith sees storms too, yet faith interprets storms differently.

The storm surrounding Peter was not imaginary. Wind remained violent. Waves continued crashing. Conditions stayed unstable.

What changed was focus.

“And when Peter was come down out of the ship, he walked on the water, to go to Jesus.”
Matthew 14:29

As long as Peter’s eyes remained fixed upon Jesus, the storm possessed no authority over Peter’s steps. The moment fear redirected attention toward surrounding chaos, the storm gained influence over perception.

“What happens when everything gets worse?”

“What happens when peace disappears again?”

“What happens when God stays silent?”

Fear magnifies waves until waves appear larger than the Savior standing above them.

Faith asks a different question.

Faith asks who remains present within the storm.

In everyday life, storms rarely disappear immediately. Bills still arrive. Reports still linger. Relationships still strain. Fear insists peace must wait until circumstances improve.

Jesus offers peace before conditions change.

Peace flows from focus,
not from the absence of waves.

Jesus was already present in the boat before the storm calmed. Presence carried authority before circumstances shifted visibly.

Fear repeatedly revisits places where Jesus already brought victory. Fear returns to the tomb searching for evidence that death still possesses authority.

“It is finished.”
John 19:30

When Jesus declared those words, fear lost legal authority over the believer. Storms may continue making noise, yet storms no longer possess final authority.

Fear focuses on the size of the storm. Faith rests within the authority of the Savior.

A sound mind does not deny reality. A sound mind refuses to enthrone reality above truth.

“Look at the waves.”

“Look at the uncertainty.”

“Look at the danger.”

Faith redirects vision upward again.

Storms reveal where attention has been trained. Fear trains vigilance. Faith trains trust.

Fear wants attention.
Jesus invites trust.

Peace becomes steady when focus remains anchored upon Jesus instead of fluctuating conditions.

Fear may continue speaking loudly during storms, yet fear never deserves the center of vision.

Jesus does.
